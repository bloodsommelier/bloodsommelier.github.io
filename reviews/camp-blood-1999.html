SOV · DIRECT-TO-VIDEO · 1999

Camp Blood (1999)

The Camp Horror Movie Made on a Bum's Tent Budget

Camp Blood, directed by Brad Sykes, is one of those dirt-cheap videos you watch with a house full of friends, everyone following the nonsense on screen while the conversation and the beer remain considerably more interesting — even if someone may never come back to your house because of it. This is an SOV slasher so starved for resources that calling it the poor man’s Friday the 13th would be a compliment worthy of putting on the box, complete with little stars underneath.

So, let’s rewind the tape with a Bic pen: young people go to a campground where, obviously, they shouldn’t go. There’s a legend about a killer clown. There are naked people wandering through the woods. There are screams. There’s ketchup. And, above all, there is the complete absence of anyone asking whether this was actually getting any good.

Since the video has to start with something, Sally Brennan (Meredith O'Brien) and Victor (Vinnie Bilancio) are in the woods when they decide to go on a naked date instead of photographing a bird famous for having the most unbearable song in the Americas. The scene wastes no time on subtlety, and what beautiful melons they have. MELONS, not apples.

Videographer Jeff Leroy was apparently so enchanted that it took him an eternity to cut away. During editing, he remained enchanted. So the whole thing stayed on the tape. But this is a micro-budget production, so young people rubbing against each other = someone about to discover that celibacy would have been the better option.

The couple is attacked by the Clown, played by Shemp Moseley — wearing a jumpsuit he apparently stole from a Michael Myers cosplayer and a rubber mask he found in a dumpster. His weapon is a machete, something included here to make it clear that Camp Blood was destined to have as many movies as Jason.

Victor is eventually dispatched, and Sally runs into the woods.

Credits.

The real story begins afterward, following Tricia (Jennifer Ritchkoff), her boyfriend Steve (Michael Taylor), their friend Jay (Tim Young), and Nicole (Betheny Zolt). The four decide to spend a weekend at Camp Blackwood, where the water isn’t crystal clear, but there is wood in the trees. The place is known to locals as Camp Blood because of the disappearances and murders that have occurred there.

Tricia, unlike everyone else, has one small evolutionary advantage: she can read. So, after checking the newspaper, she discovers that Sally has disappeared and that her car was found near the campground.

Steve, naturally, thinks everything is perfectly fine, and the movie cuts away quickly so people don’t start looking for meaning in what they’re watching. We then get two hunters who find Sally still alive, injured and covered in blood which, in some versions, I’ve been told was completely orange because apparently nobody knew how to color-correct before releasing the thing.

The clown shows up again and solves the situation using a method highly characteristic of our species:

kill everybody.

Perhaps this is the beginning of something promising.

The problem is that Camp Blood needed to fill approximately an hour with people trying to reach the place where the title says the blood is. And that is where the real horror of the video emerges:

its running time.

Some sources claim the production was originally shot in 3D, even though some home-video versions circulated in flat presentation. The curious thing is that they don’t throw anything at the camera. I don’t know if Sykes thought that was too much of a cliché or if he simply forgot to do it.

Because the production had more characters than actors, the same performers reappear in other roles.

Michael Taylor is Steve and also appears as Doctor West. Tim Young is Jay and also plays Detective Hamlin. Betheny Zolt plays Nicole and later the nurse.

“Besides being trash, we also practice human recycling.”

You watch a character die and, shortly afterward, they return for no supernatural reason whatsoever.

Even for a movie with no money to spare, I don't find the gore particularly creative, although a review from the period points out that some of the effects are better than the rest of the movie would have you expect.

In this case, the biggest problem is that between one death and another, there is a lot of walking.

A lot.

The characters walk. They talk. They walk. They stop. They walk.

And eventually you start to suspect that the real serial killer is the transportation budget, because nobody seems to have enough money to put the characters in a car and send them the hell away.

And what else is there besides that?

Despite the opening making you think we’re about to watch a video exploring the classic package of mating + woods = murder, the cliché is essentially confined to the prologue.

In the cast, Jennifer Ritchkoff is our Final Girl.

Oh, no! A spoiler!

As if it weren’t obvious within the first few seconds of the character appearing that she is the one who’s going to survive.

Maybe it’s also because she is the most functional person in the entire cast. She isn’t exactly giving an award-worthy performance; in fact, she mostly looks like nobody told her she was competing in a contest to see who could give the worst performance.

TASTING

Starter: toplessness, sex and murder.

Main course: four young people WALKING through a forest.

Sauce: bargain-bin ketchup.

Dessert: a rubber clown running around with a machete.

Aftertaste: a tape recorded by someone who discovered the REC button and decided never to stop pressing it.

Camp Blood understands the grammar of the slasher but lacks the resources to execute it with any elegance. We are not dealing with one of those “bad movies” made with postmodern self-awareness, where everyone knows they’re making garbage and keeps winking at the audience.

Here, the incompetence is genuine.

BLOOD SOMMELIER VERDICT

Camp Blood is not a good movie, but expecting it to be one would be like complaining that a gas-station hot dog doesn’t taste like duck confit.

It delivers a dirt-cheap, utterly crude SOV slasher experience packed with walking. A video for anyone curious enough to dig into the genre’s underground.

Not recommended for anyone looking for a replacement for Friday the 13th.

Highly recommended for anyone who wants to discover what happens when somebody looks at Friday the 13th and thinks:

“I can’t pull this off, but I’m doing it anyway.”

RATING — 2/5 PLASTIC KNIVES
